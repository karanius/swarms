| Threat | Effect | Current handling / residual limit |

| Observed absence | Missing B may reflect intended task differences or incomplete designs | Not a behavioral counterexample or prohibited connection |

There are no validated new composites and no evidence yet that an association-guided encoding outperforms a relational baseline.

### Candidate-stage limits

The representative audit is selected by the existing exploratory ranking, not a fresh random sample or held-out test. The 445 groups are dependent and may originate from copied designs. Configuration values are deliberately absent; declarations cannot establish runtime requirements or output activation. The viewer uses saved type/version labels and does not certify native component implementations. Whole-workflow selection may be disconnected or cyclic. Manual purpose text does not validate usefulness. Browser edits are temporary until explicit JSON download; downloading does not promote anything into the library.

### Study C limitations

The split is retrospective; the source corpus was already explored. Group hashing creates unequal numbers of graphs and neighborhoods, copied/modified designs may cross undetected, and no population-level inference follows. Native random seeds were not controlled; repeated20-second runs are stochastic observations. The accepted feature rules serialize associations; their utility does not establish richer topology learning. Masked saved-edge features measure source-pattern recovery, not behavior, necessity or the only valid design. Proposal descriptors are chosen from saved training declarations, can pool differing implementations under a type label, and are not certified native Langflow contracts. Fresh symbolic neighbors leave aliasing unresolved; acyclicity is a construction property, not a learned guarantee. Memory failure is infrastructural. No experiment executes a workflow, evaluates a user task or creates a higher plane.

### Study D limits and source-inspected counterexamples

| Threat | Consequence for interpretation |
|---|---|
| Jointly designed blueprint and tiny authored suite | Internal conformance only; no independent, representative or held-out efficacy estimate |
| Synthetic model-binding witnesses | Static grounding under assumptions, not available resources or real model behavior |
| Return annotations rather than activated output ports | Compatibility is annotation-tier evidence |
| Unknown additional intent keys lack closed-schema validation | Outside-schema mandatory constraints can be silently ignored; arbitrary requirements are not safely supported |
| Route checks establish limited local existence | No exclusive routing, shared conversation identity or general output semantics guarantee |
| Conflicting duplicate node IDs feed a last-write-wins map | Downstream details can depend on order outside the tested assertion representation |
| Revision prechecks do not resolve every pre-existing conflict | A patch may precede an ASK on an identity-conflicted state; general transactionality is not established |
| Binding identity/cardinality and malformed input contracts incomplete | Multiple positive bindings, orphan assertions and unsupported data require stronger validation |
| Oracle/protocol action ambiguity | Original acceptance differs for C17; no perfect-score claim is warranted |

These code-inspection risks were identified by the collaborating assistant and reviewed as plausible counterexamples; they are **not additional executed experiment outcomes**. The measured suite remains unchanged. The prototype is a research diagnostic, not a safe production generator. Passing type and required-field checks cannot establish task usefulness, higher-layer emergence, provider behavior or the value of the mined rules.
