# Paper6 collaboration brief — interview round 1

## Objective and working position
The user's objective is an iterative instance generator: interpret intent, descend through relevant process/flow/composite/component requirements, ground proposals in available native Langflow components and configuration, then assemble upward while revising or asking when evidence is missing. One-shot success is not required. This research profile cannot mutate the canvas; design evidence must not be presented as a deployed flow.

Paper6 currently studies a much narrower evidence-producing subsystem. Repeated local wiring and rule heads are candidate suggestions, not useful composites. Finishing the paper honestly need not mean finishing the generator. It means delivering an auditable account of what this subsystem can and cannot contribute, with a bounded, falsifiable next step toward intent-grounded generation.

## Unknowns and framing challenges
The main gap is the link from task intent to obligations and runnable configurations, not an additional diagram or a larger count of associations. The current masked-feature test measures recovery of recorded structure, not satisfaction of an intent. A different completion can be valid despite missing the masked target. Conversely, matching a saved feature need not make the proposal useful.

Native component availability/version fidelity, dynamic ports, required configuration, resource bindings, aliasing, boundary purpose, runtime behavior and revision policy remain unresolved. We have not inspected the native builder machinery or implementation in this round. Claims about adapting it require further evidence.

Section1's local-pattern research question is legitimate but much narrower than the user's objective. I would preserve that distinction rather than retroactively describe the experiment as an intent generator. Section13's 'selective proposal mechanism' is defensible only with 'symbolic, schema-level' and unresolved obligations visible.

## Proposed bounded follow-up — protocol for debate, NOT executed
**Question:** On a small fixed set of the existing symbolic proposals, can an intent-and-obligation gate distinguish evidence-supported assembly steps from steps that require a question or rejection?

**Design:** Before assessment, choose at most 12 cases by deterministic case-ID ordering, stratified across exact masked-feature recoveries and nonrecoveries, without selecting attractive examples. Retrieve their source records and training-only catalog descriptors. A coordinator supplies one explicit task intent and acceptance checklist per case, without seeing the method's recommendation; where no defensible intent can be supplied, record 'intent unavailable' rather than inventing it. Freeze intents, rubric, case list and budgets before evaluating. Compare (A) symbolic proposal alone and (B) the same proposal with an explicit obligation ledger and revision/clarification decision. This is a paired diagnostic artifact audit, not an efficacy trial.

For each arm record native descriptor/version evidence; declared and active-port knowledge; required configuration/resource bindings; fresh-versus-existing identity decision; purpose/boundary rationale; unresolved obligations; and next action (assemble conditionally / ask / revise / reject). Each pass requires a cited witness; unknown is not pass. No secrets or credentials are requested. Without a runnable native environment, runtime outcomes remain untested. Assess both arms using the same checklist; preferably use a second assessor and retain disagreements. Count unsupported affirmative claims as well as correctly exposed unknowns, so abstention alone is not treated as success.

**Primary descriptive outputs:** paired counts of unsupported affirmative claims, unresolved obligations surfaced, evidence-backed next actions, and intent-unavailable cases; all per-case ledgers and disagreements. No significance or general emergence claim. A useful result may be that almost every proposal requires clarification or that static records cannot support the audit.

**Challenge conditions:** no reduction in unsupported affirmatives; generic rather than actionable questions; inability to anchor intents independently; catalog evidence insufficient for active/configuration checks; or no difference from a simple required-fields checklist. These would weaken the claim that rule-derived proposals contribute useful grounding. This experiment cannot isolate a benefit from rule mining because both arms use the same proposals; a later baseline would be needed for that claim.
