# Study F — unchanged builder subject and independent layer interpretation

## Correction to Study E
Study E demonstrated native assembly from a supplied compact recipe. It did not measure request-to-graph selection by the normal Langflow Assistant. Its authored layer packaging remains preserved. Study F addresses that missing observation without retroactively relabeling E.

## Three distinct roles

| Role | Input | Authority | Recorded output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Generation subject | Plain authored user-level task, stock local assistant prompt and toolkit | Native headless pipeline in separate database/user workspace | Raw request, native events, persisted component graph, response, failures |
| Paper6 research assistant | Frozen raw graph and requirements, catalog/corpus evidence, layer theory | Interpret, critique and propose named boundaries; never rewrite raw subject output | Requirement coverage, assumptions, alternative groupings, proposed layer mappings, gaps |
| Coordinator/checker | Same frozen artifacts and explicit invariants | Verify source fidelity, occurrence identity, cuts, obligation and claim preservation | Recounts, failures, checked layer projection and paper evidence |

The generation subject is the existing local Langflow implementation, not pristine upstream. Its source, stock prompt/toolkit and pipeline are not modified during this experiment. Normal provider configuration uses the existing local API Proxy. Isolating process/storage or configuring a model does not change the assistant's algorithm, but must be disclosed. The normal headless API auto-applies generated graphs within its own private flow; this is not the active user canvas.

## First request and evidence gates

Freeze `request.txt` before generation. It specifies ticket-triage suggestion, a separate critique step, presentation for human consideration, no sending/updating and no execution. It supplies no component type, alias, edge, compact graph recipe or upper-plane grouping. This is one development request, not held-out or a population sample. A builder may fail or misunderstand; do not promise it produces exactly the requested result.

1. Invoke stock `/api/v1/agentic/assist/run`; record events as received and terminal errors as outcomes.
2. Retrieve the persisted private graph. Retain untouched raw output and byte hashes before analysis. No tool/prompt replacement, catalogue restriction or expected graph injection.
3. Recount native nodes/ports/edges/configuration. Separate graph-build success from requirement coverage, required resources, task behavior and human authorization.
4. Have the research assistant derive proposed composites/flow/process interpretations from observed IDs. It may use corpus templates as comparative evidence, not proof of purpose or authority. Record alternatives and no-match evidence.
5. Verify proposed lower-plane membership/ports against the original graph; export crossing edges; preserve configurations and unresolved requirements. Upper-layer status remains proposed unless a specific gate has sufficient evidence.
6. If the graph misses a requirement, a narrowly worded natural-language follow-up may be submitted to the same unchanged subject in its private flow. Preserve both graph versions and diff. Do not supply a recipe silently or rewrite the first outcome.
7. Document results and limitations in Paper6, preserving Studies A–E and the abstract. No generated task execution is authorized in this study.

## What would count as useful progress

A source-grounded graph genuinely selected by the stock assistant, an inspectable interpretation of its actual groups, and a reproducible explanation of coverage/gaps. This does not establish autonomous upper-layer generation, correct behavior, the incremental value of mined rules, generalization, or statistical significance. Source/template frequencies are observational support, not validation. The process must permit partial outputs, questions and rejection.
