# Paper 7 — business model and demand research

Research date: 2026-10-30. Status: decision blueprint; no publication, customer contact, account creation, purchase, or content-generation experiment performed by this research task.

## Decision in plain language

Build a business that helps a specific viewer achieve a specific result. Automate production around that value. Do not choose a business merely because it can produce the most videos.

The conditional starting candidate is **original practical software demonstrations**, with AI-assisted scripts/narration and locally produced, verified screen demonstrations or diagrams. This recommendation is a design-fit hypothesis based on the existing engineering context, not measured market demand. If the owner cannot independently verify the demonstrated outcome or maintain the material, choose a narrower visual-explainer candidate instead.

Do not commit to advertising alone. Ads minimize order fulfillment, while relevant affiliate offers or self-serve digital artifacts may connect a smaller audience to a purchase. None is established as the most profitable option. Compare them using the same audience, production costs, and evidence. Keep sales, support, and account administration asynchronous: no calls or meetings by design, but written support, corrections, refunds, and verification can remain necessary.

## Candidate scorecard

Scores are qualitative design judgments, not market measurements. “Strong” means promising fit with a requirement, not proven commercial performance.

| Candidate | Local-production / automation fit | Distinct audience value to establish | Revenue hypotheses | Human work and main failure | Conditional disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Practical software tutorials and reproducible workflow demos | Strong for scripts, captions, diagrams, screen capture, packaging; actual behavior needs verification | A named audience completes a real task more reliably or quickly | Ads later; relevant affiliate offers if available; tested downloadable examples | Version drift, broken instructions, security review, setup/support burden | Preferred pilot if a narrow underserved task and verifiable outcome are found |
| Computing or mathematical visual explainers | Strong for deterministic diagrams/animation; explanation quality still needs review | A specific misconception becomes understandable | Ads later; optional worksheets or visualization packs only if buyers value them | Factual sourcing, pedagogical quality, production time; learning interest need not imply buying | Alternative when visual explanation is a stronger capability than software maintenance |
| Tool comparisons / buying guides | Production can be automated, evidence gathering less so | Honest first-hand comparison under a defined use case | Relevant affiliate commissions; ads later | Paid access/testing, changing offers, conflicts and updates; fabricated reviews unacceptable | Defer unless first-hand access and maintenance budget exist |
| Generic AI narration over interchangeable stock/generated scenes | High mechanical automation | No distinctive outcome established | Advertising is merely a hypothesis | Audience indifference, weak originality, rights/policy exposure | Reject volume alone as the initial differentiator |

Suggested **candidate queries**, not measured demand: “run a private local transcription workflow,” “make searchable notes from your own recordings,” and “understand graph clustering with a visual example.” These are research probes; no audience, content promise, or business commitment follows from naming them. Avoid high-stakes advice as an initial niche: review burden would undermine the automation objective.

## Revenue architecture under the no-meetings requirement

| Path | Automated mechanism | Contact / maintenance that remains | Evidence required before choosing it | Current judgment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Platform advertising and Premium | Platform handles ad sales and revenue accounting after eligibility/acceptance | Originality/quality checks, moderation, corrections, platform/account/payment administration | Eligible account; audience that actually watches; own revenue analytics | Lowest direct fulfillment burden; uncertain time to income; zero ad income before eligibility is a required scenario [B3] |
| Affiliate recommendations | Tracking and merchant checkout can handle referred purchases | Honest product evaluation, offer checks, disclosures, broken links, attribution/returns | Program availability, approved account, product relevance, observed qualified clicks and retained commissions | A possible supplement, not assumed available for every software tool [B5–B6] |
| Self-serve digital products | Hosted checkout and file delivery can reduce repeated fulfillment | Product QA, documentation, compatibility updates, written support/refunds, piracy management | Specific buyer problem, independently usable artifact, usable payments, actual purchases later | Can fit no meetings; not zero service work [B7–B8] |
| Sponsorship or custom services | Some administration can be templated | Negotiation, approvals, custom delivery and relationship management | A separate business case | Poor initial fit with maximum automation and no meetings; not the starting assumption |

The practical design can keep revenue adapters separate: publish useful content; activate only approved, relevant monetization routes. Do not add an irrelevant purchase offer simply because it has a commission. Open-source tutorials may have no affiliate route. Digital products should provide additional reusable utility, not charge for an untested copy of the public demonstration.

## Demand measurement: questions before dashboards

| Decision question | Primary measurement approach | Honest interpretation | Important limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Is a problem searched for, and is it seasonal? | Google Trends with fixed geography, language/query wording, period, and comparator | Relative search-interest evidence [B1] | Sampled and normalized; low interest can display zero; not absolute market size or purchase intent |
| Are viewers looking for better coverage? | YouTube Studio Trends/content gaps, if legitimately available | Platform-specific idea signal [B2] | Account/audience dependence and country/language/device limits; fresh-channel access cannot be assumed |
| What already solves the problem? | Small manual sample of public original videos and official tool documentation | Existing approaches, age, scope and reproducibility | Views are historical outcomes under unknown distribution; search rank is personalized/time-dependent |
| Will people watch our version? | Later authorized publication with impressions, click-through, retention and satisfaction observations | Direct evidence about a released artifact | Cannot be learned from a private draft or search trend alone |
| Will people pay or buy through it? | Later authorized real checkout or affiliate reporting | Observed transactions, with refunds/attribution limits | Likes, views, internal enthusiasm and search interest are not payments |

No niche has been ranked using actual search-volume, CPM, RPM, conversion, or earnings data in this task. Do not turn the qualitative scorecard into a statistical chart. There is no measured distribution to chart yet.

## Low-cost validation before any publication

This protocol is proposed, not executed. Its purpose is to reject weak options cheaply; it cannot prove a profitable business privately.

1. **Freeze the decision frame.** Choose two candidate audience/job combinations, one language, a target viewing region, a research time cap, and a maximum owner review time per artifact. Record undecided inputs rather than silently selecting English or a country.
2. **Predeclare a comparable observation sample.** For each candidate, define the same number of task queries, time window, result-depth limit, inclusion criteria, and original-source deduplication rule. Choose the budget before seeing results. Save query, access date, source URL, result position, tool/content version, apparent user job, and the concrete gap being claimed. A copied video or syndicated page is not independent corroboration.
3. **Triangulate.** Compare relative Trends interest, available platform content-gap signals, and manual inspection of current tutorials. Record good existing solutions and reasons the proposed content would add nothing. A missing search result is not automatically an opportunity: it can indicate missing demand.
4. **Define one differentiated promise.** For example, a reproducible local workflow with explicit hardware requirements and failure handling. Specify exactly how the viewer verifies success. Reject broad “AI news” or “make money with AI” positioning without a defensible audience advantage.
5. **Design offline production trials.** Parent implementation may later produce matched pilot artifacts and log compute, failures, owner minutes, corrections, intelligibility, accessibility, rights, and reproducibility. This research task does not perform those trials. Internal reviewers can judge correctness and clarity; they cannot substitute for audience demand.
6. **Model uncertainty before release.** Include no-income startup, low-conversion, refund, update and platform-unavailability cases. Leave unobserved revenue parameters symbolic or explicitly assumed. Do not present a break-even calculation as a forecast.
7. **Make the next gate explicit.** Only an authorized audience-facing experiment can establish actual watching or buying. At that later gate, agree the budget and metrics before publication; keep early results separate from the data used to revise the content.

## Falsification and stop/go gates

| Claim / risk | Evidence that challenges it | Gate / consequence |
|---|---|---|
| The niche solves a specific unmet job | Current accessible tutorials already solve it well; no differentiated promise survives inspection | Revise audience/job before content production |
| Local production supports the promise | Required quality depends on unavailable hardware, unlicensed assets, hosted-only components, or unstable generation | Narrow format or stop that candidate; do not label it local end to end |
| Maximum automation is economically useful | Review, correction and maintenance dominate the predeclared time/cost ceiling | Reduce scope or choose a more deterministic format |
| No meetings is compatible with fulfillment | Buyers need recurring live setup/customization to use the proposed product | Remove custom service from the model; improve self-serve scope or reject product |
| Revenue is accessible | Jurisdiction/account/payment/rights/policy gates remain unresolved | Research-ready only; no revenue-ready claim |
| Viewer value implies a business | Released content later gets attention but no economically useful revenue signal | Revisit monetization or stop within the predeclared budget |
| Revenue implies profitability | Contribution does not cover research/review/support/maintenance and capital allocation | Do not scale output to hide a negative unit model |

“Go” at this stage means permission to investigate or prototype a bounded candidate, not proof of a profitable channel. Numerical thresholds must be chosen against the owner's budget and desired income before the corresponding test, not reverse-engineered from results.

## Economics without invented rates

Use a common accounting period and avoid double counting. Owner labor is a cost even when no cash wage is paid.

| Quantity | Model | Parameter status |
|---|---|---|
| Platform revenue | `matching view denominator × realized platform revenue per 1,000 / 1,000` | Unknown before own eligible observations; separate ads from other revenue and use correct Shorts/video denominator [B4] |
| Affiliate revenue | `qualified referrals × purchase rate × eligible commission per purchase × retained share` | All unmeasured; attribution windows, returns and rate changes are program-specific [B5–B6] |
| Product receipts | `qualified visits × purchase rate × price` | All unmeasured; taxes, fees and refunds must use actual arrangement |
| Product net contribution | `receipts − refunds − selling/payment fees − incremental fulfillment/support cost` | Platform fee schedule is observable; volumes and workload are not [B7–B8] |
| Operating contribution | `non-overlapping net revenue streams − compute/electricity − storage/bandwidth − licenses − other incremental cash costs` | Log actual costs during a later pilot |
| Economic profit | `operating contribution − owner hours × chosen labor value − allocated hardware/capital costs − other fixed costs` | Owner valuation and allocation assumptions must be disclosed |

YouTube's RPM includes more than advertising; multiplying views by a generic online “CPM” estimate is not a creator-profit calculation [B4]. Keep affiliate/product receipts outside platform RPM unless actually included in that report. Use matching currency, period and revenue denominator. Cash balance/runway and economic profit are different outcomes. Model the period before monetization explicitly, and do not assume any growth rate or time to eligibility.
